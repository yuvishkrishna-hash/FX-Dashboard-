Method

Both matrices use one window of 250 weekday sessions. Weekend bars are dropped, and any date that is missing a positive close on any of the 28 pairs is dropped. Nothing is interpolated or filled.

Returns are r = ln(Pt / Pt−1). Each ordered pair is regressed as y = a + b x. Beta is that slope. Correlation is the Pearson correlation of the two return series, and R² is the square of the regression correlation. The slope p-value is the two-sided t test. R², correlation, and the slope p-value are symmetric; beta is not. The diagonal is the pair against itself and is left neutral on the heatmap.

Log prices use ln of the same 250 closes: ln Y = a + b ln X. Beta is a log hedge ratio, an elasticity, so it is already comparable across pairs, including inverse quotes. The residual ln Y − a − b ln X is the log spread.

That residual is tested with an augmented Dickey–Fuller regression that includes a constant and chooses its lag by AIC. The p-value is the ordinary ADF p-value. It is slightly liberal next to MacKinnon cointegration critical values, and it is not a Johansen test. Durbin–Watson is computed on the same residual: values near 2 are consistent with an uncorrelated residual, and values near 0 are what a spurious regression in levels looks like.

A directed fit is marked likely spurious when R² is at least 0.5 and the residual ADF p-value is at least 0.05. ADF p and Durbin–Watson exist only on the log-price side. While either is selected, the returns heatmap stays on R². No trading signal is computed from these matrices.

Method and caveats

Each cell above the diagonal is one unordered pair, oriented as Y = row and X = column, with the row earlier than the column in the pair list. That is 378 pairs, the same upper triangle as the OLS matrix. The lower triangle is not a second estimate. Beta is not symmetric, so it is not mirrored.

The state is a random-walk intercept and hedge ratio: ln Yt = αt + βt ln Xt + et. Observation variance and the two state variances are estimated by the Shumway–Stoffer EM algorithm on the 500 weekday closes that end the day before this window, then frozen. The filter then runs through that sample and the 250-day window. Current beta is the filtered hedge ratio on the last day. Beta stability is the standard deviation of that filtered beta over the window, divided by the absolute value of its mean.

The series under test is the tradeable re-hedged spread. It starts at 0 on the first day of the window and adds Δln Y − βt|t−1 Δln X each day, using the hedge ratio known before that close. There is no look-ahead in the hedge.

The level residual ln Y − αt|t−1 − βt|t−1 ln X is the filter’s own innovation, so a stationarity test on it mostly restates the filter. Neither number is the stationarity verdict.

  • ADF uses the same constant and AIC lag as the OLS residual. KPSS is run on the level of the spread with a constant and automatic bandwidth. Statsmodels caps those p-values at 0.01 and 0.10, so a printed 0.10 means “0.10 or larger”.
  • Half-life comes from ΔSt = a + b St−1, as −ln 2 / ln(1+b). It is blank when b is outside (−1, 0), which means that AR(1) is not mean-reverting. Hurst is the slope of log standard deviation of lagged differences against log lag, for lags 2 to 20.
  • The z-score subtracts the 250-day mean and divides by the 250-day sample standard deviation. It says where the spread sits inside its own window.
  • The default table calls a pair stationary when ADF p < 0.05 and KPSS p > 0.05. ADF only drops the KPSS requirement. The “Kalman vs OLS” colours use that default Kalman rule against an ADF p < 0.05 on the static OLS log spread, fit on the same window.
  • Static beta means the filtered beta’s standard deviation is under 0.5% of its absolute mean, or the EM beta noise-to-signal ratio qβ / Ve is under 10−9. The hedge barely moved.
  • Weak hedge means the absolute filtered beta stays below 0.1 for the whole window, so the spread is close to Y alone. If that leg’s own log price also passes ADF, the pair result is mostly the leg.
